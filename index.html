Abstract

Large language models achieve strong reasoning performance, but inference strategies such as Self-Consistency (SC) are computationally expensive, as they fully expand all reasoning traces. We introduce PoLR (Path of Least Resistance), the first inference-time method to leverage prefix self-consistency for compute-efficient reasoning. PoLR clusters short prefixes of reasoning traces, identifies the dominant cluster, and expands only a subset of promising paths, preserving the accuracy benefits of SC while substantially reducing token usage and latency. Our theoretical analysis, framed via mutual information and entropy, explains why early reasoning steps encode strong signals predictive of final correctness. Empirically, PoLR consistently matches or exceeds SC across GSM8K, Math500, AIME 2024/2025, and GPQA-Diamond, reducing token usage by up to 60% and wall-clock latency by up to 50%. Moreover, PoLR is fully complementary to adaptive inference methods (e.g., Adaptive Consistency, Early-Stopping SC) and can serve as a drop-in pre-filter, making SC substantially more efficient and scalable without requiring model fine-tuning.

Do reasoning traces agree early?

Traces sampled for the same question often begin with the same words and diverge only once they commit to a set plan. In our preliminary study (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, 40 samples per question), 64% of MATH500 traces and 52% of GSM8K traces share an identical first 32 tokens, and a majority vote over just those traces gives the same accuracy as voting over all 40.

Below, the traces for one question grow token by token. Each band is a group of traces with an identical prefix so far. The band splits where the traces diverge.

Exact agreement across traces fades within a few dozen tokens, but the branches often keep describing the same plan in different words, for example: "We need to compute total meters per week. He runs 3 sprints 3 times a week" and "We need to solve: James runs 3 sprints 3 times a week" set up the same calculation. PoLR therefore compares prefixes by the words they share instead of requiring an exact match, so traces that phrase the same approach differently can still fall into one cluster.

How PoLR works

Self-Consistency samples N reasoning traces, expands every one until completion, and majority-votes the answers. PoLR adds a step in between: it generates only the beginning prefix of each trace, groups these prefixes by similarity, and expands the traces in the largest group.

  1. 1

    Sample prefixes

    Generate N short prefixes, capping each at Lp tokens (256 in our work).

  2. 2

    Cluster

    Embed the prefixes as TF-IDF vectors and group them with agglomerative clustering.

  3. 3

    Expand the dominant cluster

    Continue expanding only the prefixes in the largest cluster to full reasoning traces.

  4. 4

    Vote

    Majority-vote over the answers from the fully expanded traces

Combining with adaptive methods

Adaptive Consistency (AC) and Early-Stopping SC (ESC) stop sampling once the finished answers agree, so these can only measure agreement after traces are complete. On the other hand, PoLR filters at the prefix stage, so the two can be combined: (1) PoLR selects the dominant cluster, (2) then AC or ESC decides how many of its traces to finish. Limitations

PoLR assumes that the largest prefix cluster contains strong enough reasoning. On problems where Self-Consistency itself narrowly agrees, strong consensus over the correct path may not materialize concretely enough for PoLR to leverage it. The largest accuracy drop in the paper is on AIME 2025 with QwQ-32B: 3 of 30 problems. In all three, SC's majority held only 53–63% of the votes, and the dominant cluster was less accurate than the smaller ones. On problems like these, PoLR is not expected to outperform SC.

Related links

Self-Consistency introduced sampling many chains of thought and majority-voting their answers; it is the baseline PoLR builds on.

Adaptive-Consistency and Early-Stopping Self-Consistency stop sampling once completed answers agree. PoLR acts earlier, on prefixes, and composes with both.

Path-Consistency uses the confidence of partial reasoning to guide later generation; PoLR needs no confidence estimator or guided decoding.

UPFT showed that the first few tokens of reasoning carry strong signal and used them for training. PoLR uses the same observation at inference time, without fine-tuning.

Offloaded Reasoning (Findings of EMNLP 2025), from some of the same authors, cuts inference cost by letting a small model write reasoning that a large model refines.
